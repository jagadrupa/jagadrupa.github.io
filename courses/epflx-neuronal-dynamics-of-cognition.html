Computational Neuroscience: Neuronal Dynamics of Cognition

Audited EPFLx · edX · BIO465.2x · Wulfram Gerstner

A six-week mathematical treatment of how networks of neurons give rise to memory, perception and decision making.

Course contents by week

  • Associative memory and the Hopfield model

    How a network of neurons can store patterns and recall them from partial cues.

    Classification by similarityDetour on magnetic materialsThe Hopfield modelLearning of associationsStorage capacity

  • Attractor networks and spiking neurons

    Memories as stable states of network activity, and how the idea carries over to realistic spiking neurons.

    Attractor networksThe stochastic Hopfield modelEnergy landscapeLow-activity patternsSpiking neurons

  • Neuronal populations and mean-field theory

    Describing thousands of neurons by the activity of the population they form.

    Population activityCortical populationsConnectivityThe mean-field argumentStationary mean-fieldRandom networks

  • Perception and cortical field models

    Continuous models of cortex and the activity patterns they produce, applied to perception.

    TransientsSpatial continuum models of cortexSolution typesPerceptionHead direction cells

  • Decision making and competitive dynamics

    Decisions as a competition between neuronal populations.

    Perceptual decision makingTheory of decision dynamicsSolutionsSimulations and experimentsDecisions, actions and volition

  • Synaptic plasticity and learning

    Models of how synapses change, from short-term effects to spike-timing rules and memory formation.

    Classification of plasticityShort-term plasticityLong-term plasticitySpike-timing models of plasticityFrom spiking models to rate modelsThe triplet STDP modelOnline learning of memories
